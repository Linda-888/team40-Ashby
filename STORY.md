# Tissue Fingerprints: the locked story (10/18, five models, 188 audited patients)

Data: 188 TCGA lung cancer patients (94 LUAD, 94 LUSC, 42 tissue source sites), after an audit removed 12 of the
200 we downloaded (see "Our data audit"). Each patient has an FFPE diagnostic slide and a frozen-section slide
from a different piece of tissue. Report text comes from TCGA-Reports. Models: CONCH, MUSK, PathGen-CLIP,
QuiltNet, PLIP. All numbers are top-1 re-identification against chance ("x chance"), as ranges across the five
models.
"Hospital" means the TCGA tissue source site code. Codes are assigned per cancer type (none of ours mixes LUAD
and LUSC), so "same hospital" already implies "same diagnosis". One institution can hold separate LUAD and LUSC
codes, and some codes are tissue banks, so 42 codes is not 42 distinct hospitals.
Source: `results/figures/key_numbers.md`, regenerated by `python 11_figures.py`; the audit is
`results/duplicates.json` (`12_duplicates.py`) and `results/ink.json` (`13_ink.py`).

## Pitch (30 seconds)

Hospitals share pathology AI embeddings as if they were anonymous. Across 188 patients and five off-the-shelf
foundation models, one slide's embedding finds the same patient's slide from a *different piece of tissue*
5 to 8 times better than chance, with no training. It still holds within the same diagnosis and the same
hospital. Secret-key scrambling breaks with a few dozen known pairs, noise works only by costing much of the
clinical signal, and projecting out "identity directions" is not enough. Reports, by contrast, only reveal which
hospital they came from. Before trusting any of this, we audited TCGA itself and removed 12 pairs that were the
same piece of tissue. Slide embeddings should be treated as identifiable data.

## Our data audit (flaw slide; judges will ask)

- **Same tissue under two barcodes.** For some patients the "FFPE" and "frozen" slides are one piece of tissue:
  the same glass scanned twice (e.g. TCGA-21-1082), or the FFPE slide cut from the same piece as the frozen one
  (e.g. TCGA-44-6146, two serial sections). Matching those is matching tissue to itself.
- `12_duplicates.py` compares slide A and B thumbnails, whole and section by section, on tissue outline and on
  light/dark layout, against pairs from different patients. 12 of 200 pairs score above the other-patient 99th
  percentile on either and were removed; every analysis uses the remaining 188 (`data/patients_clean.csv`).
- Effect: Test 1 was 13-17x on all 200, 10-12x after the first, outline-only check (7 removed), and 5-8x after
  the full check (12 removed). Those few pairs were a large share of the top-1 hits, so the headline is 5-8x.
- **Pen ink.** The pair all five models matched first, TCGA-86-7711, carries the same red and green pen marks on
  both slides. `13_ink.py` drops tiles with more than 5% ink-colored pixels (1,715 of 74,896 tiles, on 175 of 376
  slides), re-averages the slide embeddings and re-runs Test 1: unchanged in every model (5-8x). Ink does not drive
  the match.
- `results/top1_pairs.png` shows every clean pair some model ranks first; by eye they are different pieces of
  tissue.

## Finding 1: slides carry an individual fingerprint (fig1_ladder)

| Candidates | Top-1 | x chance |
|---|---|---|
| All 188 patients | 2.7-4.3% vs 0.5% | **5-8x** (p<=0.004) |
| Same diagnosis | 4.3-5.9% vs 1.1% | 4-5.5x (p<=0.005) |
| Same diagnosis + same hospital (~9 candidates) | 25-37% vs 18% | **1.4-2.0x** (p<=0.007) |

- Top-10: the right patient is in a 10-name shortlist for 14-26% of patients, against 5.3% by chance.
- It works across different pieces of tissue *and* different preparations (FFPE vs frozen), with no training.
  It is not disease, and not the hospital's stain: it holds within the same diagnosis and hospital.
- Prior work trained models to re-identify (e.g. lung slides from the same surgery); we test the off-the-shelf
  models hospitals use to make shareable embeddings. A trained attacker would do better: this is a floor.
- Heatmaps (each model, 6 correctly matched patients): 96-100% of tiles point toward the true match, and the
  top 10% of tiles carry only 15-16% of the score, so the signal is spread over the slide rather than one region.
  Treat this as descriptive only, since n=6 per model.

## Finding 2: reports leak the hospital, not the patient (fig2_test2, fig4_linkage)

| Report -> slide | x chance |
|---|---|
| Zero-shot cosine in the image-text space | **0-1.0x** (no model significant; MUSK within the same hospital 1.2x, p=0.06) |
| Learned linear map, 100 training pairs from other patients | 3.4-7.1x |
| ... trained and tested on *different hospitals* | 1.2-1.6x (its shuffled-pairs control: 0.8-2.0x) |
| ... candidates restricted to the *same hospital* | **1.0-1.1x** |
| ... trained on pairs shuffled within diagnosis (control) | 0.5-1.0x |

- The plan's main hypothesis (a report finds its own slide) is **not supported** for these models.
- An attacker with paired public data can link reports to slides *at the hospital level*. Within a
  hospital, nothing is left, so the learned link encodes the site (report style and staining), not the person.
- This is still a privacy result: it says which kind of leakage exists and which does not.

## Finding 3: the obvious defenses fail (fig6_scrambling, fig5_defenses, fig3_scorecard)

- **Secret-key scrambling** (a different random rotation per release) looks safe at first: 0.8-1.2x with zero known
  pairs. With 50 known pairs, the Procrustes attack recovers 5.0-7.4x. Half of the above-chance matching
  comes back at 10-30 pairs.
- **Noise** gets Test 1 under 2x at sigma 0.5-5, depending on the model. At that point LUAD vs LUSC balanced
  accuracy has fallen from 0.80-0.88 to **0.49-0.67**.
- **Removing identity directions** (fit on half the patients, scored on the other half): Test 1 goes from
  4.0-6.5x to **2.5-5.5x** with 50 directions removed (the most a 94-patient fitting half allows), never under 2x.
  The fingerprint is patient-specific and does not sit in a small shared subspace.

## What changed from the original plan, and why (judges will ask)

- Delta's shared data had no TCGA whole slides or reports, so we downloaded 200 patients from the GDC
  (open access) and TCGA-Reports, then audited them down to 188 (above).
- We fixed two evaluation flaws found on the first 60 patients. (1) The scrambling attack had been compared
  with a baseline on a different-sized candidate pool. (2) Identity-direction removal had been fit and scored
  on the same patients, which also leaked diagnosis labels into the classifier's CV. Both now use the same
  pools and cross-fitting. The early "removal works" result disappeared once the second flaw was fixed.
- Early hints that did not survive: a 3x zero-shot Test 2 signal (60 patients), PCA breaking scrambling with
  zero pairs, and MUSK's within-hospital Test 2 hint (p=0.03 on 200, p=0.06 on 188). We report the 188 numbers.

## Limitations

- 188 patients, lung only (LUAD + LUSC); five image-text models (CONCH and MUSK via a teammate's weights).
- The audit catches same-tissue pairs it can see on thumbnails; subtler re-cuts could remain.
- Full OCR'd reports, chunked and averaged. A diagnosis-only section or morphology summary might behave differently.
- Same-hospital controls have ~9 candidates per query, so their "x chance" ceiling is low. Use the p-values.
- The subtype classifier is the only utility measure.
