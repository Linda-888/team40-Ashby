# Tissue Fingerprints: the locked story (10/17)

Data: 200 TCGA lung cancer patients (100 LUAD, 100 LUSC, 44 hospitals). Each patient has two physically
different specimens: an FFPE diagnostic slide and a frozen-section slide from a different block.
Report text comes from TCGA-Reports. Models: PathGen-CLIP, QuiltNet, PLIP (CONCH/MUSK pending access).
All numbers are top-1 re-identification against chance ("x chance"), as ranges across the three models.
Source: `results/figures/key_numbers.md` on Delta, regenerated by `python 11_figures.py`.

## Pitch (30 seconds)

Hospitals share pathology AI embeddings as if they were anonymous. Across 200 patients and three
foundation models, one slide's embedding finds the same patient's *other* specimen 13 to 17 times better
than chance. That still holds within the same diagnosis and the same hospital. Secret-key scrambling breaks
with a few dozen known pairs. Noise removes the fingerprint only once the data are clinically useless, and
projecting out "identity directions" does nothing. Reports, by contrast, only reveal which hospital they
came from. Slide embeddings should be treated as identifiable data.

## Finding 1: slides carry an individual fingerprint (fig1_ladder)

| Candidates | Top-1 | x chance |
|---|---|---|
| All 200 patients | 6.5-8.5% vs 0.5% | **13-17x** (p<0.001) |
| Same diagnosis | 7.5-9.0% vs 1.0% | 7.5-9x (p<0.001) |
| Same diagnosis + same hospital (~10 candidates) | 28-31% vs 17% | **1.6-1.8x** (p<0.001) |

- It works across different tissue blocks *and* different preparations (FFPE vs frozen). This is not
  matching one object to itself, and not just disease or stain/scanner.
- Prior work showed slide-to-slide re-identification; we confirm it under stricter controls (yardstick).
- Heatmaps (PathGen-CLIP, 6 patients): the signal is spread over almost all tiles rather than one region.
  Treat this as descriptive only, since n=6.

## Finding 2: reports leak the hospital, not the patient (fig2_test2, fig4_linkage)

| Report -> slide | x chance |
|---|---|
| Zero-shot cosine in the image-text space | **0-1.0x** (not significant) |
| Learned linear map, 120 training pairs from other patients | 5.3-7.3x |
| ... trained and tested on *different hospitals* | 1.0-2.4x |
| ... candidates restricted to the *same hospital* | **1.0-1.1x** |
| ... trained on pairs shuffled within diagnosis (control) | 1.0-1.4x |

- The plan's main hypothesis (a report finds its own slide) is **not supported** for these models.
- An attacker with paired public data can link reports to slides *at the hospital level*. Within a
  hospital, nothing is left, so the learned link encodes the site (report style and staining), not the person.
- This is still a privacy result: it says which kind of leakage exists and which does not.

## Finding 3: the obvious defenses fail (fig6_scrambling, fig5_defenses, fig3_scorecard)

- **Secret-key scrambling** (a different random rotation per release) looks safe at first: 1.2x with zero known
  pairs. With 50 known pairs, the Procrustes attack recovers 5.6-7.2x. Half of the above-chance matching
  comes back at 30-75 pairs.
- **Noise** gets Test 1 under 2x only at sigma 1.5-2. At that point LUAD vs LUSC balanced accuracy falls from
  0.77-0.79 to **0.50 (a coin flip)**.
- **Removing identity directions** (fit on half the patients, tested on the other half): with 100 directions
  removed, Test 1 is still **8.5-9.5x**. The fingerprint is patient-specific and does not sit in a shared
  low-dimensional subspace.

## What changed from the original plan, and why (judges will ask)

- Delta's shared data had no TCGA whole slides or reports, so we downloaded 200 patients from the GDC
  (open access) and TCGA-Reports.
- We fixed two evaluation flaws found on the first 60 patients. (1) The scrambling attack had been compared
  with a baseline on a different-sized candidate pool. (2) Identity-direction removal had been fit and scored
  on the same patients, which also leaked diagnosis labels into the classifier's CV. Both now use the same
  pools and cross-fitting. The early "removal works" result disappeared once the second flaw was fixed.
- Two early hints did not replicate at 200 patients: a 3x zero-shot Test 2 signal, and PCA breaking
  scrambling with zero pairs. We report the 200-patient numbers only.

## Limitations

- Three CLIP-style models. CONCH and MUSK are not run yet (gated access), and they could align text better.
- Full OCR'd reports, chunked and averaged. A diagnosis-only section or morphology summary might behave differently.
- Same-hospital controls have ~10 candidates per query, so their "x chance" ceiling is low. Use the p-values.
- Lung only (LUAD + LUSC). The subtype classifier is the only utility measure.
